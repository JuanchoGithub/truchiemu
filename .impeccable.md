## Design Context

### Users
Retro-game enthusiasts and collectors on macOS who load their own ROM libraries (NES, SNES, N64, DOS, Saturn, ScummVM, MAME, etc.). They are comfortable with emulators and expect power-user capability (keyboard/gamepad navigation, batch operations, deep settings). The job to be done: browse a personal game shelf, find a title by its cover, and launch it fast — while feeling the warmth of retro gaming.

### Brand Personality
Voice: friendly, confident, lightly playful. Three words: **Playful. Arcade. Nostalgic.**
Emotional goal: delight — the UI should feel like a love-letter to retro gaming (CRT glow, cover-art shelves, fun scanning/empty states), not a utilitarian tool.

### Aesthetic Direction
- Reference feel: Steam library, RetroArch, Playdate — cover-art grids, arcade energy, tactile controls.
- Anti-reference: flat corporate SaaS, minimalist "AI" templates, cyan-on-dark glow-as-decoration, glassmorphism-for-its-own-sake.
- The app is theme-driven (17 accent themes + light/dark/auto). Color is user-chosen and must never be overridden by design changes — accent/glow is intentional brand, not a defect.
- Keep the existing design system (`AppColors` Oklch tokens, `AppTypography` SF Rounded, `AppMotion`): warm-tinted neutrals, rounded cards, restrained motion with reduce-motion respected.

### Design Principles
1. **Delight is a feature** — empty, scanning, and download states are moments to express arcade personality, not empty placeholders.
2. **Covers are the hero** — the box-art grid is the primary surface; every change must keep covers dominant and launch obvious (hover play button, right-click/context-menu launch).
3. **Power users first, discovery second** — expose shortcuts, multi-select, batch actions; progressive disclosure hides depth without removing it.
4. **Theme is sacred** — never hardcode or alter the user's accent/glow; design within `AppColors` tokens so all 17 themes stay coherent.
5. **Keep the status-quo accessibility bar** — preserve VoiceOver labels/hints and reduce-motion handling; don't regress them.
